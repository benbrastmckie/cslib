## Key Findings

**KF1 — `Proposition Atom` is *literally* the free monad on the signature functor `Σ = {⊥(0), →(2), ∧(2), ∨(2)}`, and this is what makes `⊥` an operation (element of the algebra) rather than meta-syntax.** The Lean inductive (`Defs.lean:81-92`) is the initial algebra of `A ↦ Atom ⊔ Σ(A) = Atom ⊔ 1 ⊔ A² ⊔ A² ⊔ A²`, with the `bot` constructor occupying the `1` (nullary) summand — the *operation* side — while `atom` occupies the `Atom` summand — the *generator* side. The `Monad Proposition` instance `pure := .atom; bind A f := A.subst f` (`Defs.lean:137-139`) is exactly the free-monad structure: unit = generator-embedding, Kleisli bind = the unique Σ-homomorphism extending an atom-assignment. Because `subst`/bind is a homomorphism, the nullary-operation homomorphism law forces `subst f (bot) = bot` — this is the unconditional `| bot => .bot` clause (`Defs.lean:131`). So "⊥ is fixed by every substitution" is not a design bolt-on; it is *forced* by the universal property of the free algebra. (Confidence: High.)

**KF2 — Naturality pins down the ontological difference between `⊥` and an atom.** A nullary operation is a natural transformation `1 ⇒ Proposition` (a constant that commutes with every `Proposition(f)`), i.e. `⊥` is *natural in `Atom`*: for any `f : Atom → Atom'`, `Proposition(f)(⊥) = ⊥`. A generator `p₀` is a *component of the unit* `η` — it is precisely the thing substitution is allowed to *replace*. This is the rigorous form of Brast-McKie's Zulip #604219492 argument ("arbitrary nullary operator ≠ arbitrary atom"). The Lean witnesses are the *side-condition-free* substitution theorems: `Derivation.substAtom` (`Basic.lean:392-408`) transports `T.Derivation Γ B` to `(T.subst f).Derivation (Γ.subst f) (B >>= f)` — the Kleisli bind `B >>= f` — for *arbitrary* `f` with no `σ(⊥)=⊥` hypothesis, and `subst_preserves_minAxiom` (`FromHilbert.lean:267-280`) preserves every minimal axiom under arbitrary `f`. Under B2 both would need an `f`-restriction. (Confidence: High.)

**KF3 — B1 (fragment language) and B2 (⊥-as-atom) each break a *specific, nameable* universal property.**
- **B2 breaks the free/initial universal property.** If `⊥` were a distinguished generator, it would be a component of `η`, hence *freely assignable*: the universal property says every `g : Atom → C` extends uniquely, so `g(⊥₀)` can be any element. To keep `⊥` fixed you must restrict to the subcategory of `⊥₀`-preserving atom-maps — and on that subcategory the free-algebra universal property (which quantifies over *all* maps) no longer holds. Every substitution lemma then acquires a `σ(⊥)=⊥` side condition. This is a genuine failure of a universal property, not an inconvenience.
- **B1 breaks subalgebra-closure / changes the signature.** Dropping `⊥` for MPL is passing to the *reduct signature* `Σ⁻ = {→,∧,∨}` (Mathlib: `GeneralizedHeytingAlgebra`). MPL's free algebra then lives in a *different similarity type* than IPL's. The inclusion `Proposition⁻ ↪ Proposition⁺` is a `Σ⁻`-homomorphism, but its image is **not a subalgebra** of the `Σ⁺`-algebra (a subalgebra must be closed under *all* operations, including the nullary `⊥`, which the image omits). So MPL is not "the same algebra with fewer axioms"; the relationship becomes a free ⊣ forgetful *adjunction between distinct categories* (`WithBot : GHA → HA` left adjoint to the reduct `U`), not an inclusion. (Confidence: High.)

**KF4 — Universal-algebra reading: the whole tower is a descending chain of *varieties* (equational classes) over ONE fixed signature; leastness/explosion is EQUATIONAL, not merely quasi-equational.** With `⊥` a nullary operation, "least" is the single identity `⊥ ⊓ x = ⊥` (equivalently `⊥ ⇨ x = ⊤`, using the lattice-definable order `b ≤ a ⟺ b ⊓ a = b`). By Birkhoff HSP, an identity cuts out a *subvariety*. Therefore:

| Tier | Class of algebras | Cut out by (added identity) | Logic |
|------|-------------------|-----------------------------|-------|
| designated `bot_val` free | pointed GHA (GHA + arbitrary constant `⊥`) | — (no identity on `⊥`) | **MPL** |
| `HasLeastBot`/`HasInitialBot` | Heyting algebra | `⊥ ⊓ x = ⊥` (`⊥ ≤ x`) | **IPL** |
| canonical `⊥` from `OrderBot` | Heyting/Boolean w/ intrinsic ⊥ | (presentational — `bot_val = ⊥` forced by uniqueness of least) | IPL/CPL |
| + classicality | Boolean algebra | `¬¬x = x` (or `x ⊔ ¬x = ⊤`) | **CPL** |

So `BA ⊂ HA ⊂ (pointed) GHA` is a clean subvariety chain, each step adjoining one identity, **signature fixed throughout**. This is the exact algebraic dual of "fix one language, strengthen by adding axioms." (Confidence: High for the equational/variety classification; Medium-High for the precise "pointed GHA" framing of the MPL tier — see Evidence E4 for the one caveat.)

**KF5 — "Modularity around properties, not connectives" = the lattice of subvarieties over a fixed signature.** Property-modularity navigates the *subvariety lattice* of pointed-GHA (equivalently, the lattice of equational theories extending the base) — a single algebraic lattice with clean meet/join, where each module (explosion, classicality, and downstream linearity/etc.) is an interval. Connective-modularity (B1) instead *changes similarity type*, fragmenting the picture across a poset of signatures (a fibered structure, no single lattice). This is the decisive abstract reason to prefer property-modularity, and it is exactly why the CSLib property modules `IsIntuitionistic`/`IsClassical` (`Defs.lean:166-176`) and their semantic mirrors `HasLeastBot`/`HasInitialBot` (`BotProperties.lean:92-159`) compose additively. (Confidence: High.)

**KF6 — The critical subtlety that ties KF1 and KF4 together: "explosion = leastness = an identity" *requires* `⊥` to be a nullary operation.** If `⊥` is instead an atom/free variable (B2), then "the element named `⊥₀` is below everything" is *not* an identity in the signature `{⊓,⊔,⇨,⊤}` — `⊥₀` is not a constant of the signature, and `∀p ∀a, p ≤ a` is simply false. So under B2 the leastness constraint is *not expressible as a variety-defining identity*; it degenerates into a non-substitution-stable condition on a distinguished generator (at best a relative/parametric condition tied to that generator). **Design A is the only option under which "explosion is a subvariety step" is even a well-formed statement.** This is, in my assessment, the single strongest *new* abstract argument the report can carry. (Confidence: High.)

**KF7 — Categorical semantics of the tower: Lindenbaum-Tarski algebras are free objects; the strengthenings are reflectors (left adjoints); conservativity is faithfulness / injectivity on the free algebra.** The MPL Lindenbaum-Tarski algebra over `Atom` is the *free GHA on `Atom`* (`Proposition Atom` mod MPL-interprovability; CSLib: the Hilbert-Lindenbaum algebra, `HilbertCompleteness.lean`, `MPL.hilbert_alg_complete`); IPL's is the free Heyting algebra, CPL's the free Boolean algebra. Subvariety inclusions `HA ↪ GHA`, `BA ↪ HA` always have left-adjoint **reflectors** (quotient by the fully-invariant congruence generated by the new identity). The reflector sends free-GHA(Atom) to free-HA(Atom) on the *same* generators — realized syntactically by `Theory.intuitionisticCompletion` (`Defs.lean:198-206`, `(WithBot.some <$> T) ∪ IPL`) and semantically by the free functor `WithBot : GHA → HA` that adjoins a fresh bottom. **Conservativity = the reflection unit is injective on the ⊥-free fragment** = the induced functor is faithful there. CSLib proves exactly this: `coe_AlgEvaluate` (`FragmentGeneric.lean:165-167`) shows the `WithBot`-embedding preserves ⊥-free evaluation, and `ghaValid_iff_haValid_of_botFree` (`FragmentGeneric.lean:174-179`) is the "reflection is conservative on ⊥-free formulas" statement. `HasInitialBot` (`BotProperties.lean:149-168`) makes the categorical reading — `⊥` = initial object `0`, efq = the unique arrow `0 → a` — a first-class named artifact. (Confidence: High.)

## Recommended Approach — the framing that most strongly justifies Design A + the property-module tower

Lead the abstract-mathematics section of the report with a **single unifying picture: "one free monad, one signature, a subvariety tower."**

1. **Fix the signature `Σ = {⊥,→,∧,∨}`. `Proposition` is the free monad on `Σ`** (KF1). The universal property of the free algebra *forces* `⊥` to be an operation fixed by every substitution (KF1–KF2), because a nullary operation is a natural constant `1 ⇒ Proposition`, whereas an atom is a component of the unit `η` (a generator meant to be substituted).
2. **The logic tower MPL ⊂ IPL ⊂ CPL is exactly the descending subvariety chain (pointed) GHA ⊃ HA ⊃ BA**, each step adjoining one *identity* (`⊥ ⊓ x = ⊥`, then `¬¬x = x`) with the signature fixed (KF4). "Modularity around properties not connectives" *is* the lattice of subvarieties over that fixed signature (KF5).
3. **This picture simultaneously rules out both Design-B variants at the level of universal properties**: B2 breaks the free/initial universal property (distinguished generator is freely assignable, so `⊥`-fixing forces a subcategory where the property fails, KF3); B1 changes the similarity type (reduct signature; image is not a subalgebra, KF3), forfeiting the single-monad architecture.
4. **The knock-out punch is KF6**: only when `⊥` is a nullary operation is "explosion = leastness" a *variety-defining identity*. Under B2 the leastness constraint is not even expressible as an identity. So Design A is the unique setting in which the entire "property-strengthening = subvariety step" narrative is well-formed.
5. **Close with the categorical payoff (KF7)**: Lindenbaum-Tarski algebras are free objects, the strengthenings are reflectors (left adjoints), and *conservativity is literally the faithfulness / unit-injectivity of those reflectors on the ⊥-free fragment* — which is why "conservativity is immediate by construction," the maintainers' original ask, holds. This is also the hook for the broader programme (identity, induced orders, hyperintensionality): all further operators live over the *same* free monad, so they inherit substitution-stability for free; under B2 each would carry `⊥`-preservation side conditions.

This framing is strictly complementary to the proof-theory/Lean angle: it explains *why* the codebase's proof-theoretic modularity is forced by the abstract mathematics, rather than being a mere engineering convenience.

### Caveats / honesty notes for the report authors
- Say **"pointed GHA / Johansson algebra with a designated constant `⊥`"**, not bare "GHA," for the MPL tier (E4). Mathlib GHA has `⊤` but no `⊥`; the whole point is that `⊥` is added as an unconstrained nullary operation.
- The tiers `HasLeastBot`, `HasInitialBot`, and `canonical ⊥ (OrderBot)` are **one variety presented three ways**, not three distinct logical strengths: once leastness is imposed, the least element is unique, so `bot_val = ⊥`. The `HasLeastBot`/`HasInitialBot` split is *conceptual emphasis* (order-theoretic vs categorical), and the `OrderBot` form is a Lean-engineering convenience (Prop-mixin on an element to avoid changing the evaluator's type). Do not oversell it as a strict three-rung logical ladder.
- "Generic/fragment-generic completeness" is genuinely closed only in the **typeclass-instance sense** (`CanAlgComplete`), not as a single uniform proof (three evaluators over three algebra classes). Present it that way (E8) to stay defensible under review.
- Categorical claims (reflectors, faithfulness) are the *correct abstract reading* of what the code does; the code does not literally define the functor category — it realises the relevant unit/embedding (`WithBot`, `intuitionisticCompletion`). Frame as "the code realises the categorical content," not "the code formalises the 2-categorical machinery."
